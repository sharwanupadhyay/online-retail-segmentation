Option C: Identifying Patterns in Global E-Commerce Sales 
 

Online retail generates data at a scale that would have been unimaginable twenty years ago. 
Every click, every purchase, every return creates a record. Most of that data sits in databases 
and is never looked at carefully. When someone does look at it carefully, they often find patterns 
that change how a business operates. 
Your group will work with a large transaction dataset from a real UK-based online retailer. It 
covers millions of rows across multiple years and includes product descriptions, quantities, 
prices, customer identifiers, and country codes. The first thing you will notice is that this is 
not a clean, ready-to-use dataset. There are cancellations mixed in with purchases, negative 
quantities, missing customer IDs, and product codes that do not follow any obvious logic. 
Getting this data into a form you can analyse is itself a meaningful piece of work. 
 
Once you have clean data, think about the different questions a business might actually care 
about. Which products are the most valuable and why? Is it because they sell in high 
volume, or because they have a high value per transaction, or because they are purchased 
repeatedly by loyal customers? Are there seasonal patterns in what people buy? Which 
countries generate the most revenue, and is that just because they have more customers, or 
do customers in certain regions spend more per order? 
The main modelling task is customer segmentation using K-Means clustering. Construct 
meaningful customer-level variables that capture differences in purchasing behaviour. For 
example, you may use RFM variables — Recency, Frequency, and Monetary Value — as 
inputs to the clustering model. 
After creating the clusters, analyse what distinguishes each customer segment and what 
those differences mean for the business. A segment is only useful if it leads to a different 
action. Saying that high-value customers spend more is not an insight. Saying that a 
particular segment purchases frequently but has recently become inactive, and may 
therefore benefit from a re-engagement campaign, is more actionable. 
Seasonal sales patterns may be explored as part of your exploratory analysis, but 
time-series forecasting is not required for this project. 
Your group should also think about what this data cannot tell you. You have transactions, but 
you do not have browsing data, marketing spend, or return rates. How does that limit what 
you can conclude? 
Dataset: https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii 
 
Mandatory technical requirements: Data cleaning at scale including handling cancellations 
and missing values, construction of meaningful customer-level features such as RFM 
variables, customer segmentation using K-Means clustering, interpretation and profiling of 

the resulting clusters, at least five well-labelled visualisations that tell a coherent story, a 
business insights section with actionable recommendations, and a discussion of what the 
data cannot tell you.

1.5 Project Report Structure 
Your 2500 word report should follow this structure. Word counts are approximate guidelines, 
not hard limits. 
(a) Cover Page: Project title, group members and IDs, chosen problem statement, date 
of submission. 
(b) Executive Summary: Summarise what you did, what you found, and what the key 
takeaway is. 
(c) Introduction: Describe the problem, why it matters, and the dataset you used. 
(a) Data Description and Preprocessing: What does the dataset look like? What 
issues did you find and how did you fix them? 
(b) Exploratory Data Analysis: What patterns, trends, or anomalies did you discover? 
Reference your visualisations. 
(c) Modelling Approach: What models did you use and why? How did you train and 
evaluate them? 

(d) Results and Discussion: What did your model achieve? What do the results mean 
in the context of the problem? 
(e) Conclusion and Recommendations: What is the main insight? What would you 
suggest the business or organisation do next? 
(f) References: Cite all datasets, libraries, papers, and tools used. Use APA 7th Edition. 
Use in-text citations wherever required.
